# Random Variables & Distributions

This unit turns event-level probability into models for numerical outcomes. The central object is a random variable together with its probability distribution.

## Reading Order

1. **[Introduction to Distributions](introduction_to_distributions.md)** — discrete and continuous random variables, PMFs, PDFs, and CDFs.
2. **[Statistical Moments](statistical_moments.md)** — expectation, variance, higher moments, and moment-generating functions.
3. **[Normal Curve and Z-Scores](normal_curve_and_z_score.md)** — standardization and probability calculations with the normal distribution.
4. Study the named distribution families in **[Discrete Distributions](discrete/README.md)** and **[Continuous Distributions](continuous/README.md)**.

## Distribution Families

The discrete notes cover binomial, geometric, negative-binomial, and Poisson models. The continuous notes cover uniform, beta, exponential, gamma, normal, and log-normal models.

The Student's t, chi-square, and F distributions are intentionally **not** grouped here. They are introduced in **[Sampling & Sampling Distributions](../sampling_and_sampling_distributions/README.md)** because their main role in this repository is as reference distributions for statistics computed from samples.

## Scope Boundary

A univariate distribution describes one random variable at a time. Once several random variables are modeled together, questions about marginal distributions, conditional distributions, independence, covariance, and correlation belong in **[Joint Distributions & Covariance](../joint_distributions_and_covariance/README.md)**.
